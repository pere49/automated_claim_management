## 1. Path to CONFIRMED

A row is CONFIRMED only through §15.1 step 3: *"The row is CONFIRMED only when **every** rule the matrix marks "blocks CONFIRMED" is itself CONFIRMED, and it matches exactly one receipt."* (FACT)

The matrix in §15.3 marks these as blocking: R0, R1, R2, R3, R4, R6, R7, R10 (card claims), R11, R12. R5, R8, R9 and R13 do not block.

| # | Condition | Evidence it consumes | Single point of failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | R0 admissible | `SourceFile.status`, `ReceiptCrop.gate_status`, handwriting verdict | **The gate threshold.** §5.18: *"Combine them into a simple quality tag: good, weak, or poor, using thresholds from config."* Tuned on synthetic damage (task 5.20). A real degradation the generator does not model is tagged good or weak and passes. Also: R0's CONFIRMED condition is never written — §15.3 states only when it abstains. |
| C2 | R1 company PIN | Two independence groups reading the PIN string; **the party-role label** | **The party block detector — one component, shared by every channel.** Task 7.20: *"Write the party block detector (buyer block versus supplier block)."* Two-group agreement covers the *characters*, never the *role*. | C3 | R2 total value | Two groups; the strong-total keyword group for the doc type, **or** the totals block; vetoes | **The Money parser and the doc-type choice.** §6.2: *"parsed by one single function"*. One function, all channels. And the keyword group is selected by the doc type (S23). |
| C4 | R2 currency | A currency token within the configured distance | **The profile's symbol table.** Shared symbols (`Shs`, `Sh`, `Fr.`) belong to several countries — §7.3 lists `Shs` under Kenya, Uganda **and** Tanzania. |

| C9 | R10 statement (card) | StatementLine records; claim payment method | **There is no payment-method field on ClaimRow.** R10 can never select rows (S32). |
| C10 | R11 not a duplicate | supplier PIN + invoice + total, all CONFIRMED | **The key is three OCR reads.** One misread digit on one of two copies makes two identical receipts look distinct (S35). |
| C11 | R12 doc type known | Doc-type keywords | **R12 tests recognition, not correctness.** §15.3: *"Type is recognized."* A confidently wrong type passes (S23). |

**The structural observation.** Ten blocking conditions sound like ten locks. They are not. C2, C3, C5, C7, C11 all consume products of the *same* text model and the *same* config, computed once per crop and handed to every channel. §12.1 counts independence at the point of *character recognition* only: *"Two sources are independent only if their groups differ."* (FACT) Everything upstream of recognition — cropping, block segmentation, party-role assignment, label-to-value association, doc-type choice, amount parsing — is single-threaded. The design's independence claim protects the narrowest link in the chain.

## 2. Scenarios

Severity: **B** blocker (plausible wrong CONFIRMED in normal use), **I** important, **M** minor.

### 2.1 The test-suite blind spot that makes most of this survivable-looking

Before the table: every planted fault in §9.4 is planted **in the document or the spreadsheet**, on a receipt the generator draws correctly, and the expectation is written as if OCR reads it correctly. Examples (FACT): *"One digit of an amount changed in the spreadsheet"*, *"Wrong company PIN on the receipt (one digit off)"*, *"Invoice number changed by one character"*.

**No planted fault makes the OCR misread a correct receipt *toward* the claimed value.** (INFERENCE from the full §9.4 list.) That is the entire silent-error class. Scenarios S02–S04, S06–S11, S17 and S29 are therefore invisible to the test suite as specified, not because the tests are weak but because the fault model has a hole. This is the single most important finding in the document.

For each line of §13.6 that can produce CONFIRMED, the channels involved and what they share.

## 5. Self-attack

**A5.1 — F3 (buyer role must be positive) could destroy the tool.** On Kenyan thermal fiscal receipts the buyer PIN is often printed with a bare `PIN:` and no party block. Requiring a positively identified buyer role could push the PIN check to UNDECIDED on the majority of real receipts, and R1 blocks CONFIRMED. Combined with S46 (no invoice column), the confirmed rate could be **zero**, and a tool that confirms nothing gets switched off — which is a worse safety outcome than a tool with a known hole, because the officer returns to unaided manual checking. **Revision:** F3 becomes "buyer role positive, **or** the receipt has no party structure at all **and** the PIN sits under a label configured as buyer-typical for that document type **and** no other tax-ID-shaped string appears on the receipt". The third clause is what makes it safe: on a receipt showing exactly one tax ID, that ID is the supplier's far more often than the buyer's, so the single-PIN case must **not** confirm. I am keeping that restriction and accepting the automation cost. **Dropped:** my first draft allowed the single-PIN case to confirm when the label was buyer-typical. That is S13 with extra steps.

**A5.2 — F1 (quality floor) collides with F2.** If CONFIRMED requires a good region and F2 requires non-OCR corroboration below the floor, then the `weak` band has no confirming path at all, and F2 becomes dead code. **Revision:** F1 sets the floor at `good` for OCR-only confirmation; F2 governs `weak`, where a QR or native source can still confirm **in combination with** one OCR group. That keeps the bands distinct. Note this now depends on F11 being implemented first, or the QR leg of F2 inherits the S25–S27 weakness — **fix ordering: F11 before F2.**

**A5.3 — F11 (symmetry) creates a new silent path if implemented naively.** If a QR that cannot be decoded is treated as "no contradiction", and a QR that decodes to an unexpected schema is treated as "no fields present", then a tampered receipt with a scratched-out QR looks the same as a receipt with no QR. **Revision:** a **present but undecodable or unparseable** code is a signal in its own right — UNDECIDED, not neutral. A receipt with no code at all is neutral. The design currently cannot tell these apart; `ReceiptCrop` has no field for "a code was detected here". That is an additional record gap, and it is mine.

**A5.4 — F5 (spatial coherence) could break legitimate multi-part documents.** A hotel invoice's page 2 carries the total while page 1 carries the PIN. Requiring all fields inside one outline would refuse to confirm a perfectly ordinary two-page invoice. **Revision:** coherence is required within a **page**, and a multi-page document is one unit when its pages are consecutive in one source file and share a document identifier. Where they do not share one, abstain. **Dropped:** the stricter single-outline rule.

**A5.5 — F15 (always compute the duplicate key) contradicts F3.** If the key needs a CONFIRMED supplier PIN, and F3 makes PIN confirmation much rarer, the key is unusable far more often, and §14.2's honest rule (*"A key that cannot be read is not a clean bill"*) turns almost every row UNDECIDED through R11. **Revision:** the duplicate **key** uses the supplier PIN as *read*, not as *confirmed* — it is a fingerprint for collision detection, not an assertion about identity. A key collision is UNDECIDED (DUPLICATE); a non-collision is not a clean bill either, but R11 should block CONFIRMED only when the key is **wholly** unreadable, not when one component is merely unconfirmed. This weakens R11 relative to what I wrote in Session 1 and I want that flagged to the owner (Q3), because it trades duplicate-detection strength for usability and that is exactly the trade the governing rule says not to make casually.

**A5.6 — F12 (day/month ambiguity) will fire constantly.** Roughly 12/31 of dates have both fields ≤ 12; on a day-first profile with a validated order, insisting on DATE_UNCLEAR for all of them would send about a third of rows to the officer for no reason. **Revision:** apply F12 only when the profile is **unvalidated**, or when the claim period spans a boundary where the two readings differ materially (one inside the period, one outside). Where both readings fall inside the period and the profile is validated, confirm. **Dropped:** the blanket rule. This leaves S29 partially open on validated profiles, and I accept that consciously: the residual error is a wrong date, not a wrong amount, and it blocks nothing downstream except the statement window.

**A5.7 — F19 (show the full page) weakens privacy.** Showing the whole page to review one row exposes other receipts, other people's data, and card digits §8.5 says to keep out of storage. **Revision:** show the page **region around** the crop, not the whole page, and keep the existing rule that nothing beyond the crop is persisted.

**A5.8 — the aggregate effect of F1+F2+F3+F6+F10+F14 is a much lower confirmed rate.** I have not modelled it and cannot: the numbers depend on real receipt composition, which is UNVERIFIED. It is plausible that after these fixes the tool confirms very little, which raises Q1 as the decisive question. I am not going to trade any of these away to raise the rate, because the brief forbids it and the governing rule forbids it. The honest resolution is the form change (Q1), not a weaker policy.

## 6. QUESTIONS for the owner

**Q1. Layouts 1 and 2 cannot produce a single CONFIRMED row. Do we change the form, or change what CONFIRMED means?**
§9.2 (as amended) states *"no row on those layouts can reach CONFIRMED"*, because R3 blocks and the forms carry no invoice number. **Recommended answer: change the form.** Adopt the third layout with an invoice-number column before any real use. Reason: the alternative is dropping R3 from the blocking set, which removes the only check that ties a claim line to a specific document, and it is precisely the check that makes duplicate and substitution fraud detectable. A tool that confirms nothing is embarrassing; a tool that confirms without identifying the document is dangerous. If the form cannot change, my recommendation is that the tool ships as a **sum-checker and exception-lister only** (the P4 stopping point in §19.1) and never emits CONFIRMED at all.

**Q2. Should a receipt bearing exactly one tax ID, with no party markers, ever confirm the PIN?**
**Recommended answer: no.** Reason: on a receipt showing one tax ID, that ID is the seller's more often than the buyer's (INFERENCE, and UNVERIFIED as a frequency claim — see §7). Confirming it inverts the check the officer actually wants. The cost is that a large class of thermal receipts becomes UNDECIDED, which is the honest outcome for a document that does not identify the buyer.

**Q3. May the duplicate key be built from *read* values rather than *confirmed* values?** (Raised by A5.5.)
**Recommended answer: yes, with the collision treated as UNDECIDED and never as REFUTED.** Reason: a fingerprint used to raise suspicion has a different evidential bar than an assertion used to confirm. Keeping the confirmed-only rule makes R11 block almost everything, which will create pressure to weaken it later under worse conditions.

**Q4. Does `decisions.md` exist, and what is in it?**
No recommendation — I need the file. Findings S13, S18, S32 and Q1 may already be settled there, and if so this audit overstates them.

**Q5. Is the officer's acceptance of a REFUTED row recorded as an override in the silent-error metric?**
**Recommended answer: yes, and reported separately.** §18.1 defines the silent-error rate over rows *"the system called CONFIRMED"*, so overrides are invisible to it. Reason: the override path will carry most of the real risk once the tool is trusted, and an unmeasured last line of defence is not a defence.
